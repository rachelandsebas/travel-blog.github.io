# A change of plans

The day after the conference they had organized another tour for attendees. This one to the Colosseum and the Roman Forum. Three minutes after getting on the bus, however, the guides told us that due to the protests (same that are happening in France and across Europe) roads were blocked and we won't be visiting the Colosseum. They will do their best to drive by it so we can take photos while they tell us about the history. We immediately went up to the guides to ask if we could get off the bus so we could go on our own since we already had the tickets. To their credit, the guides were supportive and they dropped us off once we got into the city.

# The Colosseum

The Colosseum was very cool to see! Sebastian's 4th wonder of the world (Rachel's 2nd). Although the mood was ruined a bit when we learned that 400000 people were killed in the 400 years it was used. Apparently they held executions here every day. It was also really interesting to learn that the Colosseum is destroyed not so much because of its age, but because it was used as a source of materials for almost 1400 years. Apparently much of the marble of the Colosseum was used to build the Vatican and other places around Rome.

# The Roman Forum

After that we went to the Roman Forum, the central marketplace of Rome. After Pompeii these ruins were not as impressive. One arch stood out. It was built to celebrate the Romans’ success in Jerusalem and depicts the sacking of the temple in Jerusalem. The money stolen from Jerusalem was used to fund the building of the Colosseum. The Popes weren’t the first to steal from other buildings to build their own.

# The Capitoline Museum

After lunch, we went to the Capitoline Museum. The museum used to be a palace and then was converted to a museum in the 1700s. This meant that in the audio guide we often heard something like "These statues were placed here in 1740 and the room hasn't been changed since". We also gained a new appreciation for museum design because this one was clearly not designed to be an intuitive museum experience (rooms didn't have a clear theme, had to backtrack to get to the next room, etc).
