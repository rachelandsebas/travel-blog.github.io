# Capital Museum

After breakfast, we went to the Capital Museum. Walking up to the building, the signs and decorations were all about Mayan and Andean culture. Sebastián was a little disappointed to have come all the way around the world for something he could see at home. But it was just one new temporary exhibit, and the rest of the museum was on the history of Beijing.

Rachel's favourite section: I loved seeing the ancient artifacts. There was a jar for 7000 years ago and a shell necklace from between 115000 and 9500 years ago (not sure how they couldn't narrow that range!). It's so cool to think of the people who would have made and used this items.

Sebastián's favourite section: He enjoyed the modern section and how techy the museum was. He liked the exhibit on the olympics. Beijing is the only city to have hosted both the Summer and the Winter Olympics! He was also impressed by a graphic showing the significant improvements in the city's air quality in the last decade.

For both of us, it was fascinating to notice the careful wordings in how certain events were explained, such as the "Chinese resistance to the War of Japanese Aggression."

# Cooking Class

This evening we did a cooking class. We were able to walked there from our hotel. The class was in the hutongs (alleyways) in a beautiful neighbourhood. The teacher walked our group of 6 through cooking three dishes:

1. Spicy Potato Salad (Lang Ya Tu Dou): French fries coated in various sauces/spices and topped with spring onions and coriander.
2. Malatang Peicai: A spicy soup with vegetables, tofu, shrimp, and beef.
3. Spicy Wonton (Hong You Chao Shou): wontons coated in chili oil and topped with spring onions and coriander.

The food was delicious and we really enjoyed the class. To chopped the vegetable we used Chinese clevers, which Sebastian has been wanting to buy while we where here. Unfortunately we got to the end of our time here without being able to make it to a shop. Thankfully the class was selling them, so we didn't hesitate to get one!
