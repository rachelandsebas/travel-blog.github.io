# Natural Whetstone Museum

Our destination was the Natural Whetstone Museum where we had a knife-sharpenning class booked! Our instructor started by showing us two knives that were purposely damaged under a microscope. Then she guided us through sharpening the knives by using progressively smoother whetstones. At the end, we got to see our knives under the microscope again, and we could how much of a difference it had made. We also got to use the knife to slice a sheet of paper and it went through super easily. We may have also spent a bit of money to purchase a knife and whetstone to bring back home. Our instructor was surprised we didn't already own a whetstone (I think it's super common here).

# Arashiyama Monkey Park

From there we went to Arashiyama Monkey Park. We walked up a steep hill and near the top there is an area where there are a lot of Japanese macaques. Rachel absolutely loved it. Walking up to the main clearing we could see lots of monkeys climbing trees, running around, or just sitting on the path. At the top, there is a building you can enter and feed the monkeys from. Inside you can buy food and then feed the monkeys through the fenced windows. The monkeys were adorable and it was fun to see their different personalities. There were even a few mothers with little babies. The babies were noticeably less coordinated than the adults - making them even cuter.

# Nishiki Market

For dinner, we headed to a large market area. I've followed Guga foods for a while, and they made a video on must try items at this market. With a bit of detective work, we found the shops Guga visited and got to try Wagyu and Kobe beef. Tomorrow we will try some more recommendations :). 

::: carousel
![](images/109_nishiki_1.mp4)
![](images/109_nishiki_2.jpg)
![](images/109_nishiki_3.jpg)

The above cuts of beef are quite pricey, so we only had small pieces. After, we went to Ichiran for ramen. The restaurant is unique in that is optimized for minimal contact. You ordered from a machine, you get assigned a cubicle table where the food is delivered through a curtain, and they provide small signs you can hand to the staff for common requests.
