Rachel here! I wanted to share a few additional observations and experiences from our time in Korea, China, and Japan - things that didn't fit into other parts.

# China

Things we really liked:
- Public Transit: In both Shanghai and Beijing, the subways had over 20 lines and were easy to use.
- Signage: Signage was super readable and almost always had both Chinese and English. Starging multiple blocks from a subway entrance there were signs showing where to go for each line. Also, street signs showed not just the name of the road but also whether it goes North/South or East/West and the building numbers included on that block. Good signage was the thing we missed the most after getting to Japan (and Japan was still not bad).
- Gardens: Neither of us had realized how green China was! The cities had trees and beautiful (free) gardens everywhere (including a flower garden in the middle of a subway station).
- Schools: Sebastián was especially impressed by Shanghai High School where the IMO was held. Students are able to participate in all kinds of incredible opportunities, including every after school activity you can think of as well as starting businesses and conducting research.
- Affordability: Food and activities were super cheap. We got a large dinner for 2 at an 8 times Michelin guide restaurant for 10 CAD total (5 CAD each).

Other notes on China:
- Very visible security and policing: There were cameras everywhere and it was very common to see a police booth on the side of the road. You also have to pass security before entering any subway station. We went through less security to enter the Japanese parliament building!
- Jobs: We noticed a lot of people doing jobs that seemed...less neccesary. At every subway station there were 4-5 security people when only 2 were really needed. On the train between terminals at the airport, there was an attendent in each train car whose job seemed to be to push the "door open" button. On the other hand, we didn't see any homelessness, so maybe this approach works. Either way I'd love to understand the economics of it better.
- Foreigners: While there were some, we didn't see a ton of foreigners, and you can tell that they aren't as used to seeing foreigners. We would ocassionally notice kids starting at us and we were both approached multiple times by people asking if they could take a photo with us. Among the foreigners we did see we were surprised that Spanish-speakers were probably the biggest language group (followed by English and Russian).

# Japan

Things we really liked:
- Cleanliness: There was basically no littering. The hotels were also super clean, even the budget places we stayed at.
- Gardens: I loved the Japanese style of garden. Compared to China, they were more of a mossy green with a focus on trees and water.
- Good food: The food was delicious! We especially liked the beef and seafood.
- Toilets: They had the fanciests toilets we've ever seen: heated seats, bidets, deodorizers, and more. This was a welcomed change from China where many places had squat toiltest with no toilet paper.

Other notes on Japan:
- Eating and Walking: Culturally in Japan it is not ok to eat while walking. When you buy food, you either eat it in front of the store or you carry it home/to a bench and eat it there. This was not my favourite rule. It was at times inconvenient to not be able to snack on the way to an activity.
- Shopping: There were malls everywhere! Our favourite though were the markets - the one in Kyoto in particular had tons of food, clothes, souvenirs, all along covered walkways.

# The Cities Ranked

Below is our ranking of the cities we visited. This is based just on our experience there, so I'm sure some things would change if we had more time to experience them (2-5 are all very close).

1. Shanghai - our favourite! Great transit, beautiful, good food, affordable.
2. Tokyo - great food, lots of activities, good transit.
3. Seoul - modern, good transit, good food.
4. Kyoto - beautiful, good food, good activities.
5. Beijing - awesome transit, crowded.
6. Hiroshima - amazing museum + garden, ok transit.
7. Osaka - busy and crowded.

All in all we had an amazing time and were very grateful for all that we saw, learned, and experienced.
